# Historical dogfooding audit — six independent products

**Decision/evidence scope:** 2026-11-24 · retrospective, not a release or new product. All six products are represented. This audit records observed use of tools in their own development and each other's development, including negative outcomes. No seventh coordinator is required.

## Verdict

**Practical usefulness is demonstrated.** The record contains 22 scoped cases, 21 distinct pinned source files and 20 distinct historical successful Actions runs. 13 distinct directed cross-product operational relationships are supported within the six products (excluding self-use, external BART and audit-feedback rows). This is a conservative verified sample, not every task or every possible product pairing. Six products have evidence of self-use or self-validation; four show actual self-use workspaces or release workflow, two show product-specific self-validation. Do not convert the latter into claims of published self-archives or finished artwork.

Evidence of a functioning engineering feedback loop is distinct from measurement of relative productivity. Retain the prior controlled benchmark (7/8 both arms) without using it to dismiss the historical evidence.

## Evidence classification

- Self-use: tool records or qualifies its actual development.
- Self-validation: product validates its own documented workflow/kit; not necessarily full production adoption.
- Integrated: cited product or permanent-branch PR merged; no implication of a new release.
- Adopter-use: actual durable project-local output or validated execution, even if not published to product main.
- Verified-draft: real reviewable outputs, no integration claim.
- Feedback: adopter audit/pilot created upstream defect or reusable knowledge; not necessarily automatically found by a CLI.
- External-adopter: useful real project case outside the six products.

## Important engineering outcomes

1. **Regressions:** Brand Identity adopted the Lydite Vault, converted five expected failures into ordinary regression cases, and merged a real approval/evidence-link fix (#16). Veraphify's Vault ran 211 named cases and covered five pre-release fixes (#57–61), including a real Graphify export of a dot-prefixed repository. Lydite's own release CI rejects changed archive modes and bytes. This shows guards exist and were exercised, not a counted estimate of future incidents avoided.
2. **Cross-product feedback:** Lydite #121 led to Provenistem #4. Upstream audit probes independently confirmed three defects; the public successful run denotes successful reproduction, not repaired behavior. Provenistem #2–4 remain open at audit intake. The same duplicate-key weakness is independently recorded for Brand Identity #25; the similarity is a repair opportunity, not proof of shared causation. Brand #26 tracks a Lydite receipt-verifier backport. Existing issues are preserved; no duplicates created.
3. **Knowledge persistence:** Provenistem's own catalog, VIB's ledger, archived source citations, a version-specific README draft, and the reusable five-step documentation technique survive sessions. BART's ledger identifies two actually merged development checkpoints. Agent harness trial is narrower than general autonomous recovery.
4. **Bidirectional loops:** Provenistem ↔ Veraphify (catalog and pinned audit/graph evidence; Provenistem map/navigation work remains adopter-specific); README HQ ↔ Veraphify (documentation validation and reusable technique derived from the pilot; archive adoption); Lydite → Brand Identity → Veraphify, with Brand Identity approval records and Veraphify validation. These are observed complementary contributions, not a required dependency chain.
5. **Missing evidence:** no record found proving every directed pair (30 possible), every tool used for every task, final Veraphify artwork, or quantified reduction in token use/time/regressions. No need to manufacture missing relationships to satisfy independence. A repository containing a blank template or draft does not alone count as practical use.
